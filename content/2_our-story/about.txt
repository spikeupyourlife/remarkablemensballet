Title: Our story

----

Headline: How this happened

----

Body:

It started, as most regrettable ideas do, in a pub.

Doug Kamo and Simon Green had an idea. History does not record whether anyone tried to talk them out of it. What we do know is that the Remarkable Men's Ballet has been part of the Queenstown Christmas Show since the very first one (2006, we're fairly sure; the paperwork was kept in a pub).

We have survived every rebrand: the Shotover Jet Remarkable Christmas Spectacular, the Shotover Christmas Cracker, and today's Queenstown Christmas Show. Names change. Tutus endure.

Our members are tradies, professionals, a councillor, a police constable, a volunteer firefighter, a newspaper journalist and, on one memorable occasion, the mayor. Almost none of us are trained. That is the point.

By 2009 we were 30 strong, and Mayor Clive Geddes stepped in as an eleventh-hour replacement ballerina to a rock version of Beethoven's Fifth. In 2011 Mayor Vanessa van Uden stole our flowers, and was wrapped in tinsel and carried offstage for her trouble. In 2013 we took the show outdoors at the Queenstown Recreation Ground with red lipstick, hairy legs and a rugby ball, and the paper said we stole the show. We have not given it back.

In 2025 we left the Christmas Show for the first time, crossing the frontier to the Arrowtown Autumn Festival with a routine to Vivaldi's *Autumn*. They had us back in 2026. Our touring radius is now roughly 20 kilometres.

This December is our twentieth year. We take it very seriously. Mostly.

Every cent the Christmas Show raises goes to Queenstown families and kids who need it. That bit we take seriously all the way.
