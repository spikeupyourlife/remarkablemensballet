Title: Join the Troupe

----

Headline: Auditions are (technically) open

----

Blurb: The Remarkable Men's Ballet is always looking for men of courage, questionable flexibility and a robust sense of humour. No experience necessary. Actually, experience is slightly discouraged.

----

Requirements:

- Must be a man of Queenstown Lakes (or able to pretend convincingly)
- Must own, or be prepared to borrow, a sense of humour
- Ability to touch toes: optional. Ability to point at toes: required
- Must commit to rehearsals in the weeks before the Christmas Show (dates announced closer to the time)
- Facial hair welcome. Leg hair is a matter for your conscience
- Must understand that the tutu chooses you, not the other way round
- Must keep a straight face for at least four consecutive bars

----

Notrequired: Rhythm, coordination, dignity, prior ballet training, a working knowledge of French.

----

Process:

1. Send us an email with your name and your single best dance move (described in words; we are not ready for video)
2. Attend a rigorous audition, which is mostly a cup of tea with Ferg
3. Get measured for a tutu. Try not to cry
4. Rehearse. Rehearse. Forget everything on the night. Be magnificent anyway

----

Faq:

-
  q: Do I need any dance experience?
  a: No. Experience is slightly discouraged. Our choreographers have spent years proving you don't need it.
-
  q: Do I have to be a man?
  a: It's in the name. Our choreographers are the honourable exception, and they outrank all of us.
-
  q: Do I have to live in Queenstown?
  a: Queenstown Lakes, or able to pretend convincingly. Arrowtown counts. We've checked.
-
  q: How much time does it take?
  a: Rehearsals in the weeks before the Christmas Show, then the show itself. In 2026 that's Saturday 5 and Sunday 6 December. Rehearsal dates are announced closer to the time.
-
  q: What do I wear?
  a: A tutu. We'll sort it. You will be measured. Try not to cry.
-
  q: Are the tutus washed?
  a: Next question.
-
  q: Will my mates see this?
  a: More than 2,700 people saw us last Christmas, so yes.
-
  q: Is it for a good cause?
  a: Yes. The Christmas Show raises money for Queenstown families and kids, including the Salvation Army, Happiness House, Baskets of Blessings and the Buddy Programme.
-
  q: I'm a mayor. Is that a problem?
  a: Historically, no.
